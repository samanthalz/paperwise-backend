| Project Summary          | The domain of computer science is experiencing an unparalleled increase in research publications, rendering it very difficult for researchers to remain informed, navigate extensive literature, and digest intricate material efficiently. Computer science ranks third globally in research production and demonstrates substantial growth, as indicated by a 47% rise in published articles from 2016 to 2022 and exponential growth in preprint submissions. This "information overload" creates considerable cognitive difficulties. Researchers face challenges in locating key information and deciphering specialised terminology, complex mathematical equations, and interdisciplinary concepts, in addition to the cognitive strain of integrating data from multiple sources and the psychological burden of feeling consistently inadequate. This project seeks to tackle these significant difficulties by creating PaperWise. |

| 1. What is the purpose of the project? | The main objective of the project is to tackle the considerable difficulties encountered by academics and students in managing the rapidly increasing volume of academic literature, especially in Computer Science. This domain undergoes a "hyperbolic increase" in research papers, resulting in information overload, diminished understanding due to specialised terminology and intricate concepts, and challenges in prioritising pertinent studies. Current AI technologies, although beneficial, frequently provide erroneous or "hallucinated" material, lack critical analysis, and necessitate manual verification, hence failing to completely optimise the research workflow. PaperWise aims to mitigate cognitive loads and improve efficiency by offering a more dependable and thorough AI-driven solution for the study of academic publications. |

| 2. What are the planned or desired outcomes?   | To implement a web-based LLM system that automatically generates concise summaries and extracts key points from Computer Science research papers, helping users quickly understand core insights without reading the whole paper.   |

| 3. Explain your research design               | The project uses the Waterfall Model as its primary research framework. This decision is based on the model's organised, sequential methodology, which offers transparent and orderly advancement through the software development lifecycle. Waterfall Model guarantees that each phase is meticulously finalised and confirmed prior to progressing to the subsequent phase. This linearity helps in managing the project's complexity, offers predictable milestones, and promotes compliance with the project's deadline and deliverables.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| 4. Outline the principal methods you will use | The primary methodologies utilised in the PaperWise project correspond with the organised stages of the waterfall model. Requirements: A user survey was conducted using Google Forms, to obtain both quantitative and qualitative data from the academic community. This study collected information about how researchers work, the challenges they face with too much information, their use of current AI tools, and specific features they want, like automatic summarisation, key point extraction, personalised recommendations, and contextual question- answering. Open-ended questions were also asked for essential feedback regarding user concerns and preferred functionalities. Design: A use case diagram was designed to define the system's boundaries and user interactions, whereas a system flowchart illustrated the whole logical flow of the application's procedures from backend to frontend. These visual models enabled thorough comprehension and precise articulation of the system's architecture. Furthermore, user interface prototyping was employed to create intuitive and user-centric interfaces. Development: This phase entailed the development of web- based applications. Fundamental strategies encompassed the application of advanced Natural Language Processing (NLP) |

| techniques for effective text extraction from research papers. The system incorporates large language models (LLMs) to facilitate functions such as concise summarization, key point extraction and question-answering function to ensure that responses align with the specific content of the paper. A personalised recommendation engine will be included by utilising paper information and citation patterns to provide relevant literature. Testing: Techniques for measuring the system's efficacy encompass user feedback surveys (employing Likert scale ratings) to evaluate the perceived utility and user experience of functionalities, such as personalised recommendations. Accuracy benchmarking of the LLM-generated responses from the system will utilise ground-truth answers from known question answering datasets to ensure the dependability and precision of the system's outputs. Deployment &Maintenance: The deployment aspect primarily involves making the application accessible, which is achieved through deployment on Vercel. While this phase typically considers ongoing functionality and support, the emphasis within the scope of this academic project is on demonstrating core viability and functionality rather than extensive long-term maintenance or operational support.   |
